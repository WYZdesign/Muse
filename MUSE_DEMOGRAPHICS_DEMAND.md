# MUSE — DEMOGRAPHICS, AUDIENCE & DEMAND

> Who Muse is for, what they look like, and where the demand comes from. Grounded in the product's
> actual type/style taxonomy (`components/types.ts`) and `STRATEGY.md` (market validation). This is the
> "who is this for + is there a market" answer for a boardroom.

---

## 1. The core demographic: three distinct buyers

### A. Behind-the-camera creatives (crew) — the sellers
Photographer, Director, Videographer, Editor, Writer, Producer, Designer, MUA, Stylist.
- **Pain:** finding reliable, willing talent securely; getting paid without chasing/ghosting; being
  discovered beyond Instagram's reach.
- **Behavior:** currently collab through FB groups + DMs + Venmo; juggle paid gigs + TFP portfolio builds.
- **Muse's hook:** booked sessions with escrow + verified talent + a review trail they can send to brands.

### B. In-front-of-camera creatives (talent) — the subjects
Model, Actor, Content Creator, Influencer, Dancer, Musician.
- **Pain:** being scouted safely without sketchy DMs; knowing boundaries/consent before showing up;
  getting paid / building portfolio.
- **Behavior:** want to be seen (see brand core — "be seen, be made into art"); risk-averse about
  one-on-one shoots they find unsolicited.
- **Muse's hook:** verification, signed consent/disclosure, trusted contact, and the platform being a
  *safe* place to be found — the thing they can't get from a cold DM.

### C. Industry / buyers — the demand side (the richest)
Casting Director, Producer, Art Buyer, Fine Art Agent, Creative Director, Studios, Brands.
- **Pain:** finding *trustworthy* talent at scale; no-shows, damages, scammers.
- **Behavior:** have budgets ($2k–$8k briefs, $80–$160/hr hires); currently source via agencies or risky
  cold outreach.
- **Muse's hook:** pre-verified, pre-qualified talent + built-in safety/payment. This is the **Muse
  Studio** ($29.99/mo) tier.

## 2. The two-sided duality (the product insight)

Muse is built on a **creative duality** — behind-camera and in-front-of-camera need each other.
Looking-for logic surfaces the *opposite* side first:
- A photographer (behind) sees Models first. A model (front) sees Photographers first.
- This creates mutual demand: supply and demand are the *same people choosing each other*.

**Same-side** collab (photographer + photographer, director + director) is always available as a second
group. This is how a single invite creates both a market and a community.

## 4. Where the demand comes from (market validation)

### The existing (broken) market = proof of demand
The demand is **already proven** — it's huge and it's currently unmanaged:

- **Facebook groups:** TFP (trade-for-portfolio), collabs, paid shoots, mixer events — all at massive
  scale, with **zero verification, zero consent, zero payment protection.**
- **Subreddits + DMs + Venmo:** the same work happening informally, with the same risks.
- **Peerspace:** rented spaces by the hour, but does **not** verify talent, handle consent, or settle
  talent payments. Nobody owns the **trust + transaction** layer for shoots.

### The founder's unfair assets (demand you already have)
- **FD Photo Studio** — anchor partner; physical supply + credibility (founder was a Chicago manager).
- **FD Photo Mixers** — an existing **LA / NYC / Chicago** offline two-sided community that already
  meets IRL. Seeds the marketplace; almost no marketplace ever launches multi-city.
- **Firsthand domain pain** — sees the scam/flake/safety problems weekly in the scene.

### The expansion ladder
1. Modeling + photography (the FD authority wedge).
2. **Studio/space network** — FD first, then partners. **Apex Photo Studios** (Downtown LA) and **Hubble Studio** (Arts District) are listed in the app as aspirational partner studios with market-rate pricing — not officially partnered yet, but real LA studios Muse would like to onboard. Partner studios expand supply without owning space.
3. MUA, stylist, musician, adjacent creative services.
4. **"First-shoot normies"** — consumer "safe first shoot" (headshots, couples, creator days) — the
   mass market.

## 5. The demand timeline (honest framing)

The validated demand is **for the transaction + trust layer**, not yet for "Muse the app." Nobody is
measuring "I wish I had a verified booking app" — they're measuring "I post in a FB group and it's a
minefield." So the *real* demand signal is:
- People already doing this work (the FB/mixer scene) → supply that converts immediately.
- The transaction + safety layer → the product that makes them switch.

**Measure transactions, not signups** — bookings closed, dollars moved, repeat rate. (See `STRATEGY.md`.
The timing thesis — creator economy, IRL is back, "anti-AI real + verified" — is pitch narrative, not
measured data.)
